# The distant nebulae: from shells to masses

*Study of look 1 going to look 2, 2026-09-27/28. Tool: `tools/blind` (`--dial`,
`--toward distant`). Sheets and keys are kept outside the repository.*

The shell is retiring (`ROADMAP.md`, *The shell's retirement, and bubbles*), and
the distant nebulae were shells. They had to be remade as masses, and needed not
to match the shells but to look good and in keeping with the main nebula. Four
blind rounds, each the same four sheets: seeds 3, 7 and 12 with the sky opened
(`open` at 1 on both sides, where distant nebulae show), and seed 12 as its seed
made it. Each pair was look 1's distant shells against the masses of the day,
seen toward the distant nebulae the main one hides least; the scorer said which
they would rather have.

**What they are for.** In a default mass sky a distant nebula is almost always
hidden: on seeds 3 and 7 every one was 0-8% clear of the main nebula. They matter
where the sky opens, and in the bubbles and open compositions to come.

| round | masses preferred | shells | tied | the scorer's notes, in short |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | no clear edges; they "fade out into nothing" like a piece cut, feathered, from a larger nebula; too faint; "distant" yet laid over the nebula |
| 2 | 0 | 1 | 3 | very round, "cookie cut"; soft edges vanish in front of the main gas; dark pieces read like the dust given up; too bold |
| 3 | 4 | 0 | 0 | the shape is better, but it lacks solidity: not enough information from light |
| 4 | 4 | 0 | 0 | excellent form; a little too bright |

What each round changed:

1. **Masses, as the main one is made.** Made so, they were dark silhouettes -- the
   cluster within an opaque mass -- so they were thinned, given a glowing cavity,
   blown partly open, unsqueezed, and softened at the edge. The softness was the
   mistake round 1 named.
2. **A boundary and a depth.** The glow ended at the mass's lumpy inner surface
   with a bright rim just inside it, the ionisation front; the outline was lobed;
   and each bubble's light was veiled by the galaxy's dust in front of it
   (`Bubble::veil`, `distant-dust`) -- a depth cue that applies to any object
   placed in the sky, for the bubbles to come. The dust layer the galaxy was
   tuned with is thin for this -- an optical depth of 0.005-0.16 over the
   distances involved -- so it is scaled.
3. **Glow alone.** At a few degrees across, a mass's lumps were specks reading as
   dust and its outline a circle. So a distant nebula became ionised gas and no
   dark gas: filaments and knots in its light, a hard bright rim, an outline lobed
   well over a third either way and squeezed along its axes. The sheets' second
   view had been aimed at nebulae 80-100% behind the main gas; it now looks only
   at ones at least half clear.
4. **Lit from beside.** Lit from within, a nebula seen from outside glows evenly
   and has no form. So its clusters go outside it, round toward the side as seen --
   from behind it would be a silhouette, from in front a flat disc -- and it has a
   body of gas again, without the fine lumps, with only a little glow through it
   (`distant-density` 1, `distant-cavity` 0.008). Lit faces, shaded folds: a cloud.

After round 4 the clusters, moved outward, are brightened by the distance they
moved and not its square: as bright at the heart as from within made them too
bright beside the main nebula. That is the state look 2 takes.
