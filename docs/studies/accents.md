# Accents: open clusters

*Look 10, 2026-09-30. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-29-open-clusters-design.md`
and its addendum. Tools: `atlas --toward accents --game 1`. The sheets are kept
outside the repository.*

The first of the sky's accents, and the ground every later one stands on: open
clusters, placed where the galaxy's young stars are born. Judged at a game's
resolution -- 1920 by 1080, 75 degrees across, from a 2048 skybox, aimed at a
sky's largest cluster -- over four galaxies (a barred spiral, a lenticular, a
grand design and a flocculent spiral), with the nebula on and off. The first
sheets, at 45 degrees and half a game's resolution, hid them: the user asked for
game-resolution views and for the clusters alone, and every round after was
judged that way.

## What each round changed

1. **Solid white balls.** Every member of a real cluster drawn on the field
   stars' scale, which holds only the brightest of any population. Now a few
   tens drawn, the faint majority a haze.
2. **Lost in the band.** `accent-near` even at 0.8 drew near-uniformly in
   distance to 8 kpc, so most clusters lay kiloparsecs off among the band's
   stars. Only a near cluster stands out -- its members among the brightest
   stars about it -- so the dial now runs to strongly near, even in log
   distance at 0.75.
3. **Not visible enough.** Young clusters' stars made as luminous as the field's
   young stars; more members; a fuller haze. The user: *if they aren't visible,
   they aren't worth having*.
4. **Too large, the haze over the top.** A dense core of a few parsecs; the haze
   to 30% of the members' light. Associations -- tens of parsecs of scattered
   young stars -- dissolved into the field from near by and are parked.
5. **A pasted patch.** A cluster a sixth of the screen's height read as the
   sticker `PRINCIPLES §4` refuses. Never nearer than its stars spread to about
   3 degrees, 1/15 of a 75-degree view's height (the user's bound).
6. **A random patch, not a cluster.** Photographs of open clusters, looked at for
   structure only, showed a brightness hierarchy, a core thinning into a halo,
   and a haze that is structured -- patchy glow cut by dust in young clusters'
   birth clouds, wisps about the Pleiades' bright stars -- never a round halo.
   Members now run down to a tenth of the field's faintest, the young boost for
   the massive few; two thirds in a Plummer core; and the haze textured by
   noise and tinted by age: reddish, then blue, then faint. A textured glow
   (the user's choice) over a real small nebula, which is the fallback.
7. **Buried.** Several clusters lay behind the galaxy's dust. A candidate is now
   kept by how much of its light reaches the observer: 82% of it on average,
   against 58%.
8. **One cluster, or fifteen.** The count followed the young disc in reach:
   a sky between spiral arms, with no young stars about it, had one; a rich
   arm fifteen. Now held between half and double the dial wherever there is
   disc in reach. No sky is promised a near one.

## Where it stands

In the last round the user found the clusters good on eight of twelve views.
The rest have causes the model gives, and were accepted: a lenticular's clusters
are old and faint; one lay behind the main nebula, which placement does not
avoid; seed 11's observer sits between arms, its clusters all far. **No blind
A/B was run**, at the user's direction, after ten rounds of mock-ups: look 10 is
the first look change made without one, and says so.
